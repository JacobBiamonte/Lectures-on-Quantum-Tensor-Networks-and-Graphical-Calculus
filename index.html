A diagrammatic language for quantum information.

These lectures give a self-contained, graduate-accessible route through tensor networks as a language for quantum states, operators, channels, and processes.

The manuscript develops tensor notation and graphical reasoning before moving through matrix product states, Boolean and stabilizer tensors, entanglement invariants, open quantum systems, and counting by contraction.

Seven parts

  1. 01From Tensors to Networks
  2. 02Matrix Product States
  3. 03Boolean Tensor Networks
  4. 04Symmetries and Stabilizer Tensor Theory
  5. 05Tensor Networks and Entanglement Invariants
  6. 06Tensor Networks for Open Quantum Systems
  7. 07Counting Solutions by Tensor Contraction

Appendix Algebra on quantum states, XOR algebra, Karnaugh minimization, and tensor products.

The research line behind the lectures.

The programme begins with ground-state spin logic and exact Ising embeddings. Its Boolean tensor-network language is then developed by translating logic gates into tensors and function composition into contraction.

  1. Jacob D. Biamonte, Non-perturbative k-body to two-body commuting conversion Hamiltonians and embedding problem instances into Ising spins, Phys. Rev. A 77, 052331 (2008). DOI

    Exact Hamiltonian constructions and Ising embeddings of Boolean problem instances.

  2. James D. Whitfield, Mauro Faccin, and Jacob D. Biamonte, Ground-state spin logic, EPL 99, 57004 (2012). DOI

    Boolean truth tables embedded in ground-state subspaces, with gates combined into larger spin-logic constructions.

  3. Yves Lafont, Towards an algebraic theory of Boolean circuits, J. Pure Appl. Algebra 184, 257–310 (2003).

    An algebraic and categorical account of Boolean-circuit structure.

  4. Jacob D. Biamonte, Stephen R. Clark, and Dieter Jaksch, Categorical Tensor Network States, AIP Advances 1, 042172 (2011). DOI

    The explicit tensor-network step: logic gates become tensors and composition becomes contraction.

  5. Ville Bergholm and Jacob D. Biamonte, Categorical Quantum Circuits, J. Phys. A 44, 245304 (2011). DOI

    A categorical tensor calculus for finite-dimensional quantum circuits and their graphical rewrite rules.

  6. Jacob Biamonte, Ville Bergholm, and Marco Lanzagorta, Tensor Network Methods for Invariant Theory, J. Phys. A 46, 475301 (2013). DOI

    The graphical treatment of polynomial invariants used in the lectures’ entanglement-invariants material.

  7. Christopher J. Wood, Jacob D. Biamonte, and David G. Cory, Tensor Networks and Graphical Calculus for Open Quantum Systems, Quantum Inf. Comput. 15, 759–811 (2015). DOI

    Graphical representations of states, channels, measurements, and open-system processes.

  8. Jacob D. Biamonte, Jason Morton, and Jacob W. Turner, Tensor Network Contractions for #SAT, J. Stat. Phys. 160, 1389–1404 (2015). DOI

    Boolean counting problems expressed and evaluated as tensor-network contractions.

  9. Jacob Biamonte and Ville Bergholm, Tensor Networks in a Nutshell (2017).

    The concise companion tutorial to the present lectures.

Selected foundations; the complete bibliography remains in the manuscript.
